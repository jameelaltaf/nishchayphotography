The studio

Three people, one camera bag each, and a rule about not getting in the way.

Our approach

Watch first. Direct second. Never both at once.

Most of a wedding day doesn't need a photographer's opinion. It needs someone paying close enough attention to be in the right place ninety seconds before anything happens.

So that's most of what we do — read the room, anticipate, stay out of the frame. Then, for the twenty minutes that genuinely benefit from a hand, we direct properly: where to stand, where the light is, what to do with your hands. Clear, quick, and finished before it starts to feel like a photoshoot.

The edit follows the same logic. Warm, true-to-skin colour, film-leaning contrast, and no trends that will date the gallery in three years. What the day looked like, slightly better lit.

Practical things

The boring details that actually matter

  • $2M commercial general liability insurance — certificates sent directly to your venue
  • Dual-card-slot bodies, backup bodies and lenses on every job
  • Files backed up to two physical drives plus encrypted cloud storage the same night
  • Registered Ontario business; HST-registered and invoiced properly
  • Written contract with an illness-and-emergency clause before any money changes hands
  • Client data handled under PIPEDA — see our privacy notice

Where we work

Toronto first, Ontario often, elsewhere when it's worth it.

The studio is in Liberty Village, open by appointment for portrait sessions, album viewings and planning meetings. Most of our work happens elsewhere: Distillery District and Evergreen Brick Works in the city, Mississauga and Brampton banquet halls, Niagara vineyards, Muskoka lakefronts and Prince Edward County barns.

We take on a small number of destination weddings each year — if you're planning something outside Canada, ask.
