A February elopement on Toronto Island

Twelve guests, minus-nine windchill, and the calmest wedding day we've photographed in years.

Maya and Chris booked us eleven weeks out, which is roughly ten weeks and five days later than most of our couples. They'd cancelled a 180-guest reception, kept the date, and moved the whole thing to a rented cottage on Ward's Island with twelve people and a thermos of chai.

The forecast, when we checked it the night before, said minus nine with a windchill closer to minus sixteen. The ferry schedule said four crossings. We told them we'd bring hand warmers and shoot fast.

Why winter light is worth the cold

Here's the thing nobody mentions about February weddings in Toronto: the sun never gets high. From about 10am to 3pm you get a long, low, raking light that in June only exists for forty minutes at either end of the day. Every frame outdoors that morning had that quality, and none of it required waiting.

We planned for eight minutes outside at a time. In the end we did four sets of eight minutes and had more portraits than most full-day weddings produce.

The trade-off is the clock. Sunset was at 5:58pm, and civil twilight ended twenty-eight minutes later. Everything outdoors had to happen before four. We built the timeline backwards from that and it held.

The timeline we actually ran

  • 10:15am — ferry across, cottage getting-ready coverage begins
  • 12:30pm — first look on the boardwalk, eight minutes outdoors
  • 1:00pm — ceremony indoors, fourteen minutes start to finish
  • 1:30pm — family portraits, indoors, five groupings
  • 2:45pm — couple portraits along the shoreline in three short bursts
  • 4:00pm — long lunch, speeches, nobody looked at a watch again

What we'd do differently

Two things. First, we'd have brought a second pair of gloves each — the thin ones you can actually operate a camera in soak through in about ninety minutes, and there is nowhere on Ward's Island to buy replacements in February.

Second, we'd have scheduled the family portraits before the ceremony rather than after. Everyone was warm, dressed, and in one room at 12:45pm. By 1:30pm three people had wandered off to find the kettle.

If you're planning a winter elopement in Toronto

  • Check the ferry schedule twice. Winter service is reduced and weather-dependent. Build in a full crossing of slack.
  • Book indoors for the ceremony. Outdoor vows below minus five are a photograph, not an experience.
  • Plan your outdoor time in short bursts. Eight minutes, warm up, repeat. It works far better than one long push.
  • Start earlier than feels necessary. Losing the light at 4pm changes everything downstream.
  • Have somewhere warm to put coats. Sounds trivial. It's the single most-photographed logistical failure at winter weddings.

Maya and Chris got their full gallery in four weeks. They've since told us that three of the twelve guests have printed the same photograph — the one on the boardwalk, taken in the second eight-minute burst, roughly ninety seconds before everyone gave up and went inside.
